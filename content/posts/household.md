## Household hints and tips

### Ants

Inevitably, the ants showed up. In the kitchen. And the pantry. And the cupboard above the sink. They seem to be coming in through two tiny gaps between the bench top and wooden cupboards.

A quick search suggests these are the Black House Ant, or _Iridomyrmex glaber_, an ant species native to Australia. According to the ACT Government, "these are small black ants 2.5-3 mm long. They nest in rockeries, near or under paths, in wall cavities and in potted plants. They eat a variety of foods and are attracted to sweet substances. They give an annoying bite but do not sting." Well, that's something.

The ACT Government also suggests our best bet is to use a chemical control. Initially, a bait at the site with some insecticide. And, failing that, a commercial spray on the nest. Hopefully, that won't be necessary.

So, after some searching, borax was acquired from our local supermarket. Borax (sodium borate, tincal or tincar) is a crystalline borate mineral containing Boron. Though originally found in dry lake beds in Tibet, it's now mined in California. As well as being used in pest control, it's also used as a cleaning agent in laundry produts, in gold mining and in welding. These days it's used in ceramics and glazes, anti-fungal treatments and flame retardants. There is some concern about total exposure to Boron and whether it can have reproductive health and respiratory implications. Oddly, it was also used as a food additive, to impart a firm, rubbery texture in things like noodles. That's now banned in Australia, the United States, China, Thailand and a number of other countries.

A bit more searching and some borax was mixed at a 1:1 ratio with some icing sugar and enough water added to make a paste. Two lots were popped on lids from the recycling at the two entry points hand we'll see what happens. In theory, the Borax (and some of the sugar) will be carried back to the nest, spreading the poison.
